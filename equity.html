Support for Underrepresented Students

Many students face barriers related to cost, access, or lack of industry exposure. This page highlights programs designed to support first generation, low income, and underrepresented students who want to pursue aviation.

1. Financial Support

What it means: Flight training is too expensive for most first-generation and underrepresented students as FAFSA/Pell Grants generally do not apply to standalone Part 61/141 flight schools only when the program is part of an accredited university. As a result, students often rely on large private loans or airline-sponsored financing programs to afford training. Solution Income-Based Flight Training Loans Pay after you earn Modeled after medical/law ISAs. Students pay $0 during training and repay only once they earn a pilot salary (ex after becoming a CFI or regional FO).

3. Exposure to Aviation Careers

What it means: Programs can offer tours, workshops, simulator experiences, and classroom visits that show students what a pilot career actually looks like. Increasing early exposure helps students understand the path and builds confidence Solutions Bring pilots into schools similar to how the military visits high schools to speak, and inspire. Partner with school districts to offer dual credit aviation courses, reducing early training costs and giving students a head start.
